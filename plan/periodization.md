## 1. The three goals, and how they are sequenced

You named three targets. They are not equally compatible, so they are sequenced rather than pursued at once:

| Goal | Where it is trained | Why there |
|---|---|---|
| **Fatigue resistance / durability** | Every block, rising through all 39 weeks | The slowest-adapting quality and your actual race limiter. It is never absent from the plan. |
| **FTP** | B2 (Sep–Oct) and B5 (Feb–Mar) | Concentrated blocks at full fuelling. You cannot build watts in a deficit. |
| **Body composition** | B4 only (Nov–Jan) | Placed at the lowest-intensity block, far from any FTP push. |

**The rule that governs everything:** never chase watts and kilograms in the same block.

## 5. Strength training — placement and progression

You asked whether strength goes on off days or easy ride days. **Off days, with one exception.**

**The reasoning.** The interference effect — strength work blunting endurance adaptation, and vice versa —
is largely a function of proximity and glycogen state. Heavy lower-body work done within ~6 h of a quality
ride compromises one or both. The practical rules:

1. **Never** put heavy legs the day before a VO2max or threshold session. Neuromuscular fatigue lasts 24–48 h
   and you will simply fail the intervals.
2. **Never** put heavy legs the day before the long ride.
3. **Do** put the heaviest session on Monday, immediately after the weekend's long rides. You are already
   fatigued, so you are not costing a fresh day, and you get the full week to recover before Saturday.
4. If you must combine with a ride on the same day, ride **easy in the morning, lift in the evening**,
   with 6+ h between. Never lift before a quality ride.

**Schedule.** Starts September as you planned, 2×/week through March, dropping to 1×/week in B6:

| Block | Frequency | Placement | Emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 (Aug) | 1× | Fri evening | Movement quality, learn the lifts, light loads |
| B2 (Sep–Oct) | 2× | Mon (A, heavy) + Fri (B, light) | Build to 85 % 1RM |
| B4 (Nov–Jan) | 2× | Mon (A, **heaviest**) + Thu (B) | Maximal strength — this is the lean-mass protector |
| B5 (Feb–Mar) | 2× | Mon (A) + Fri (B) | Maintain load, reduce volume |
| B6 (Apr–May) | 1× | Mon (B only) | Maintenance. Do not chase PRs here. |

**Strength A — heavy, 45–55 min**
- Back squat or trap-bar deadlift — 4×4 @ 85 % 1RM
- Bulgarian split squat — 3×6/leg
- Romanian deadlift — 3×6
- Single-leg calf raise — 3×10
- Pallof press + side plank — 3 sets

**Strength B — lighter, 30–35 min**
- Step-up — 3×8/leg
- Hip thrust — 3×8
- Scapular retraction / face pull — 3×12
- Wrist flexor & extensor eccentrics, forearm supination — 2×12 *(median-nerve prophylaxis — this matters
  more for you than any leg exercise, given ultra-distance hand numbness)*
- Dead bug, bird dog — 3 sets

Heavy strength during a caloric deficit is the **main lever protecting lean mass**. Do not drop it when you
start cutting in November — that is exactly when it earns its place.

## 6. Nutrition and body composition

### Build and Specific blocks (energy-neutral)
- Carbohydrate 6–8 g/kg/day, more on long days
- **90–110 g carbs/h on all rides > 2 h.** The gut is a trainable organ and is a genuine limiter in
  multi-day racing — train it on every long ride, not just the important ones
- Protein 1.6 g/kg/day

### B4 only — the deficit block
- **Deficit 250 kcal/day, expressed as ~1750 kcal/week.** Take it on rest and easy days, never on a quality
  session or long ride.
- Protein raised to 1.8–2.2 g/kg/day
- Expected rate ~0.23 kg/week → **~2.8 kg over the 12-week block (80.5 → ~77.7 kg)**

**The 76 kg target doesn't fit inside B4.** At 0.23 kg/week, 80.5 kg → 76 kg is a 4.5 kg loss —
**~19.6 weeks**, not the 12 weeks B4 actually has. B4 alone gets you to ~77.7 kg; the remaining
~1.7 kg needs either a lower B4 target (accept ~77.7 kg and pick this back up in a later block) or
a longer deficit window past B4 — **not a bigger deficit.** 250 kcal/day is already set by the
RED-S ceiling below; do not raise it toward 300–400 to compress the timeline, which is exactly
what v1 got wrong.

**Why 250 and not 300–400.** At ~69 kg fat-free mass, exercise expenditure across this block's volume range
(7–14.6 h/wk) leaves a hard ceiling: a deficit above **~256 kcal/day** drops energy availability below
**30 kcal/kg FFM/day**, the threshold associated with relative energy deficiency — suppressed hormonal
function, impaired bone turnover, blunted adaptation. v1's 300–400 sat above that line for 12 straight weeks.
Note also that because the deficit is a fixed daily number, EA is *identical* at 7 h and 14.6 h — so express
it weekly and let it flex with volume.

**Stop-loss — pause the deficit if any two appear:** resting HR up > 5 bpm for a week · HRV trending down ·
sleep quality falling · repeated failure to complete UF01 at prescribed power · low mood or libido ·
recurrent URTI · ferritin or testosterone drifting down on bloods.

### The two corrections, in detail

**UF04** used 30 s @ 125 % / 15 s @ 50 % FTP. Weighted across the 45-second cycle that is a set mean of
**exactly 1.00 × FTP** — a threshold session wearing a VO2max name. The entire mechanism of the short-interval
format is that microrecoveries let you hold a *supra-threshold* set average while repeatedly re-loading oxygen
uptake. Now 133/55, set mean **1.07 × FTP**.

**UF09** ramped at 14 W/min while applying the FTP = 0.75 × best-60 s estimator, which is calibrated to a
**20 W/min** ramp. A shallower ramp means more time before failure and a lower peak 1-minute power, so it
under-read FTP by roughly 3–6 % — and every other workout is anchored to that number. Now a true 20 W/min
ramp from 100 W. **Run this first**; expect to fail near 380 W, ~14 min into the ramp.

## 10. The non-training levers

From your own race debrief, ranked by expected time saved over a 2600 km / 31000 m parcours. Worth more
than the 3 kg:

1. **Stopped time** — 30 min/day less ≈ 4.5 h. Largest single lever by a wide margin.
2. **Sleep strategy** — shifting one full sleep to a 90 min roadside bivvy on the right night = 3–5 h.
3. **Gearing** — a 1:1 or lower bottom gear (34×34/36, or a 46/30 sub-compact) turns 11 % ramps from a
   survival grind into a sustainable Z3 effort, and reduces cumulative muscular damage over nine days.
4. **Night riding in heat** — riding 02:00–08:00, sleeping through the 14:00–17:00 furnace.
5. **Body mass** — 3 kg ≈ 60–90 min. Real, but fifth on the list.
